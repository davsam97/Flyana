## Being in orbit means falling… without ever hitting the ground

Picture a cannon on top of a very high mountain, Newton's thought experiment. A ball fired gently falls near the foot. Faster, it falls further. At a certain speed, the Earth curves away beneath it **as fast as it falls**: it never touches the ground again. It is in **orbit**.

A satellite is thus in **permanent free fall**. That is why astronauts float in the International Space Station, although gravity there is still about 90 % of its value on the ground: the station and they fall together.

## Gravitation

The force that bends the path is Newton's gravitation:

$$
F = \frac{G\,M\,m}{r^2}
$$

where $r$ is the distance to the Earth's **centre**. Orbital mechanics uses the **gravitational parameter** $\mu = G\,M$ directly, known far more precisely than $G$ and $M$ separately. For the Earth:

$$
\mu_\oplus = 3.986 \times 10^{14} \text{ m}^3/\text{s}^2
$$

The satellite's mass $m$ drops out of the equations: **a feather and a lorry on the same orbit travel at the same speed**.

## Kepler's three laws

Johannes Kepler drew them, early in the 17th century, from Tycho Brahe's observations of Mars. Newton later derived them from gravitation.

1. **Orbits are ellipses** with the Earth's centre at one **focus**. The nearest point is the **perigee**, the furthest the **apogee** (around the Sun: perihelion and aphelion). A circle is a special case of an ellipse.
2. **The law of areas**: the line joining the Earth to the satellite sweeps out equal areas in equal times. As a result, the satellite moves **fastest at perigee** and slowest at apogee.
3. **The law of periods**: the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis $a$:

$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}}
$$

The period depends **only** on $a$, not on the shape of the ellipse nor on the satellite's mass. Doubling $a$ multiplies the period by $2^{3/2} \approx 2.83$.
