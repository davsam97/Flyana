## Space agencies

A space agency sets programmes, funds them and hands their building over to industry.

- **NASA** (United States, 1958): the largest civil space budget in the world, from the Apollo missions to the James Webb telescope and the Artemis lunar programme.
- **ESA** (Europe, 1975), headquartered in Paris: more than twenty member states jointly fund launchers, science, Earth observation and exploration. The **geographical return** principle shares industrial contracts between countries in proportion to their contribution.
- **CNES** (France, 1961): the French national agency, which runs the **Guiana Space Centre** in Kourou and leads its own programmes, often in cooperation.
- Other major agencies: **Roscosmos** (Russia), **CNSA** (China), **JAXA** (Japan), **ISRO** (India).

## Industry

Satellites and launchers are built by industry: in Europe, **Airbus Defence and Space** and **Thales Alenia Space** for satellites, **ArianeGroup** for the Ariane launchers, **Avio** for Vega. **Arianespace** markets and operates the launches. In the United States, private companies such as **SpaceX** have shaken up the sector with reusable first stages.

## Europe's spaceport: Kourou

The **Guiana Space Centre**, in French Guiana, lies at about 5° north. It combines two strengths:

- **closeness to the equator**: the Earth's rotation gives eastward launches about 463 m/s, and the geostationary orbit needs little plane change;
- **the Atlantic Ocean to the east**, over which trajectories pass without flying over inhabited areas.

## Ariane 6

Ariane 6 took over from Ariane 5, retired in 2023. It first flew on 9 July 2024. Its architecture:

- a **core stage** powered by the **Vulcain 2.1** engine, burning liquid hydrogen and oxygen;
- two or four **P120C solid boosters**, depending on the version: **Ariane 62** or **Ariane 64**;
- an **upper stage** powered by the **Vinci** engine, also hydrogen and oxygen, and **re-ignitable**. It can shut down and restart several times, to drop satellites on different orbits during one flight, then deorbit itself so as not to leave debris.

The P120C motor is shared with the first stage of the small **Vega-C** launcher: sharing parts between launchers cuts costs.
