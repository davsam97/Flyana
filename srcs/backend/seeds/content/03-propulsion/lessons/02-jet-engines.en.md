Aircraft engines are **air-breathing**: they take their oxygen from the air. They carry only fuel, which makes them very economical.

## The turbojet

A turbojet goes through four steps, the **Brayton cycle**:

1. **Compression**: a multi-stage bladed compressor multiplies the air's pressure (by 30 to 60 on recent engines).
2. **Combustion**: kerosene is injected in the combustion chamber; the air heats up to more than 1,500 °C.
3. **Expansion through the turbine**: the hot gases spin a turbine, linked by a shaft to the compressor. It takes just the energy needed to drive it.
4. **Exhaust**: the remaining energy accelerates the gases in the nozzle, which produces thrust.

## The turbofan

Remember: for the same thrust, you can accelerate a lot of air a little, or a little air a lot. Accelerating **a lot of air a little** wastes less energy, since the kinetic energy lost in the jet grows with the square of its speed. That is the principle of the **turbofan**.

A large **fan** at the front, driven by the turbine, sends most of the air **around** the engine's core, without burning it. The **bypass ratio** is the ratio of that cold flow to the hot flow through the core. It exceeds 10 on recent airliner engines: most of the thrust then comes from the fan. These engines are thriftier and quieter than pure turbojets.

Fighters, which must fly fast, keep low bypass ratios and often add an **afterburner**: fuel is injected again behind the turbine for extra thrust, at the cost of a high fuel burn.

## The turboprop

For short, slow flights (below about Mach 0.6), the idea is taken further: the turbine drives a **propeller**, which accelerates a very large mass of air. The **turboprop** is the most economical at low speed.

## The ramjet and the scramjet

At very high speed, air is compressed just by being slowed in the inlet: no compressor or turbine is needed any more. That is the **ramjet**, a simple tube with a combustion chamber. It only works at high speed, around Mach 2 to 5, and **cannot start from rest**: another engine must bring it up to speed.

Above about Mach 5, slowing the air down to subsonic speed would heat it too much. The **scramjet** (supersonic combustion ramjet) therefore burns its fuel in a flow that stays supersonic. It is a technical challenge still at the experimental stage.
