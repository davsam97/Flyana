## Anatomy of an airfoil

Cut a wing perpendicular to its span and you get an **airfoil**.

- The rounded **leading edge** meets the air.
- The thin **trailing edge** lets it go.
- The **chord** is the segment joining them.
- **Camber** measures how curved the airfoil is. A cambered airfoil lifts even at zero angle of attack. A symmetrical one lifts only with an angle of attack, which suits aerobatics or control surfaces.
- The **upper surface** and the **lower surface** are its two faces.

## Lifting more at low speed: high-lift devices

At take-off and landing the aircraft flies slowly. By $L = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S C_L$, it then needs a larger $C_L$. **Flaps** at the trailing edge and **slats** at the leading edge add camber, sometimes area, and delay the stall. Maximum $C_L$ thus goes from about 1.5 to nearly 3, at the cost of more drag.

## The Reynolds number

The flow around a wing also depends on how inertia compares with the air's viscosity, measured by the **Reynolds number**:

$$
Re = \frac{\rho\,V\,c}{\mu}
$$

where $c$ is the chord and $\mu \approx 1.79 \times 10^{-5}$ Pa·s the dynamic viscosity of air at sea level. For a 1.5 m chord at 50 m/s, $Re \approx 5 \times 10^6$. A smaller wind-tunnel model does not have the full-size aircraft's $Re$: one of the difficulties of testing.

## When air compresses: the transonic regime

Below about Mach 0.3, air can be treated as incompressible. Faster than that, its density varies through the flow. And because the air **speeds up over the upper surface**, it reaches Mach 1 locally while the aircraft still flies around Mach 0.75 to 0.8. The flight Mach number at which this happens is the **critical Mach number**. A **shock wave** then forms on the wing, drag rises sharply ("drag divergence") and the flow can separate behind the shock.

Airliners push that threshold back in two ways:

- **sweep**: a wing angled backwards only "feels" part of the speed, its component perpendicular to the leading edge;
- **supercritical airfoils**: a flatter upper surface that slows the air less abruptly.

## Supersonic flight

Above Mach 1, the aircraft outruns the pressure information it creates. Disturbances can no longer travel ahead of it and pile up into oblique shock waves, the source of the sonic boom. Supersonic aircraft have thin leading edges and strongly swept or delta wings, like Concorde, which cruised at Mach 2.02.
